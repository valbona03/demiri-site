How to Prevent Frozen Pipes in a Chicago Winter (Homeowner's Guide)

Updated November 2026·By Demiri Installs & Repairs·5 min read
Quick answer

To prevent frozen pipes, keep your thermostat at 55°F or higher around the clock, let faucets on exterior walls drip on the coldest nights, open sink cabinet doors so warm air reaches the pipes, insulate exposed pipes, and disconnect garden hoses. Most important of all: make sure your furnace is working before the deep cold hits.

Chicago-area winters regularly bring stretches of single-digit and below-zero temperatures, and that's when frozen and burst pipes become a real risk. Water expands as it freezes, which builds pressure inside the pipe until something gives. A burst pipe can soak floors, drywall, and belongings fast. The good news: a few simple habits prevent most freeze-ups.

Which pipes are most likely to freeze?

Pipes freeze where cold air reaches them and warm air doesn't. In suburban Chicago homes, the usual trouble spots are:

  • Pipes running along exterior walls, especially under kitchen and bathroom sinks
  • Pipes in crawl spaces, unheated basements, attics, and garages
  • Outdoor spigots and the lines feeding them
  • Any pipe near a drafty spot, like a rim joist, dryer vent, or gap in the foundation

What temperature should you keep your house at?

Keep the thermostat at 55°F or higher at all times, including overnight and when you travel. A deep nighttime setback might save a little on gas, but it lets the coldest corners of the house drop well below the thermostat reading. If you have a smart thermostat, check that no schedule is setting the temperature lower while you're away.

What can you do yourself to protect your pipes?

  • Let faucets drip. On the coldest nights, a thin, steady drip from faucets on exterior walls keeps water moving and relieves pressure.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so warm room air can reach the pipes.
  • Insulate exposed pipes with foam pipe sleeves from any hardware store. They're cheap and quick to install.
  • Seal drafts near pipes with caulk or spray foam.
  • Disconnect garden hoses and shut off and drain outdoor spigots if you have an interior shutoff valve.
  • Know where your main water shutoff is so you can close it fast if a pipe bursts.

Why does your furnace matter for frozen pipes?

Your furnace is your pipes' first line of defense. If it quits on a below-zero night, the indoor temperature can fall quickly, and pipes in outside walls are the first to go. That's why it pays to have the furnace checked before the coldest weeks, change the air filter regularly, and watch for warning signs like short cycling, odd noises, or rooms that won't warm up. If you're leaving town for a few days in winter, ask someone to check that the heat is still on.

What should you do if a pipe freezes?

If a faucet gives only a trickle or nothing at all in freezing weather, a pipe is probably frozen. Here's what to do:

  • Leave the faucet open so water can flow as the ice melts.
  • Warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer, a space heater kept a safe distance away, or warm towels. Start near the faucet and work back.
  • Never use an open flame, such as a torch or propane heater, to thaw a pipe.
  • If you see a crack or leak, shut off the main water right away, then call a pro.

Frequently asked questions

What temperature should I keep my house to prevent frozen pipes?
At least 55°F at all times, day and night, even when you're away. Go a little warmer during a severe cold snap.
Should I let my faucets drip in cold weather?
Yes. On the coldest nights, a thin, steady drip from faucets on exterior walls keeps water moving and relieves pressure if ice forms.
How do I know if a pipe is frozen?
A faucet that gives only a trickle or no water in freezing weather is the most common sign. Keep it open, warm the pipe gently, never use an open flame, and shut off the water if you see a crack.
What should I do if my furnace stops working in freezing weather?
Check the thermostat, breaker, filter, and furnace door. If the heat doesn't come back, call for emergency furnace repair right away. A cold house can put pipes at risk within hours.
