No Hot Water? Common Water Heater Problems & Fixes

Updated November 2026·By Demiri Installs & Repairs·4 min read
Quick answer

No hot water usually comes down to one of four things: a tripped breaker, a tripped reset button on an electric heater, a pilot light that's gone out on a gas heater, or a failed heating element or thermocouple. Check the breaker and reset button first; if that doesn't fix it, it's time for a pro.

Few things ruin a morning faster than a cold shower. When the hot water disappears, the cause is usually fixable — and sometimes it's something you can check yourself in a minute. Here's how to narrow it down.

First, check the power or pilot

Electric water heater

  • Breaker: Make sure the water heater's breaker hasn't tripped. Reset it once.
  • Reset button: Electric heaters have a red reset button near the thermostat panel. If it's popped, press it back in — this often restores hot water immediately.

Gas water heater

  • Pilot light: If the pilot has gone out, there's your answer. Relight it following the instructions on the tank label. If it won't stay lit, the thermocouple likely needs replacing.

Not enough hot water (but not none)?

If you're getting some hot water that runs out fast, the likely causes are a failing heating element, sediment built up in the tank, or a thermostat set too low. Try setting the thermostat to about 120°F. Persistent short supply usually means a worn element or a tank that needs flushing.

Is a leaking water heater an emergency?

Check where the water is coming from. A drip from a fitting or the pressure-relief valve is often a simple repair. But water coming from the body of the tank typically means internal corrosion — and a cracked tank can't be fixed, only replaced. If you see tank leakage, shut off the water supply to the heater and call a pro before it floods.

Repair or replace?

A good rule of thumb:

  • Repair if the unit is under ~8–10 years old with a minor fault (element, thermostat, valve).
  • Replace if it's 10–15+ years old, leaking from the tank, or repair costs approach half the price of a new unit.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I have no hot water?
On electric heaters, a tripped reset button or failed element; on gas, a pilot light that's out or a bad thermocouple. Check the breaker and reset button first.
Is a leaking water heater an emergency?
A fitting or valve leak is often repairable, but a leak from the tank body means the tank is failing — shut off the supply and call a pro.
Should I repair or replace my water heater?
Repair if it's under ~10 years old with a minor fault; replace if it's 10–15+ years, leaking from the tank, or the repair costs near half a new unit.
How long do water heaters last?
Tank units typically last 8–12 years; tankless longer. Annual flushing helps, especially with hard water.
