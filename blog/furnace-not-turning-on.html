Furnace Not Turning On? What to Check First (Chicago Homeowner's Guide)

Updated November 2026·By Demiri Installs & Repairs·5 min read
Quick answer

If your furnace won't turn on, check four things first: the thermostat (set to HEAT, above room temp, fresh batteries), the breaker (reset it once), the air filter (replace if dirty), and the furnace door panel (it must be fully closed). If there's still no heat — or you smell gas — call a licensed HVAC pro right away.

A furnace that won't fire up is one of the most common service calls in the Chicago suburbs, especially once temperatures drop below freezing. The good news: a lot of "no heat" problems come down to a few simple things you can safely check yourself in a couple of minutes. Here's the exact order to work through.

1. Is the thermostat the problem?

Start here — it's the number-one cause of a furnace that seems dead. Make sure your thermostat is:

  • Set to HEAT (not COOL or OFF)
  • Set a few degrees above the current room temperature
  • Running on fresh batteries — dead batteries can shut the whole system down and look like a major failure

Also set the fan to AUTO, not ON. Leaving it on ON runs the blower constantly — which can feel like the furnace is "blowing cold air" even when it's working fine.

2. Has a breaker tripped?

Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker labeled for the furnace. Flip it fully off, wait 30 seconds, then back on. If the breaker trips again right away, stop — that points to an electrical fault that needs a professional, not another reset.

3. When did you last change the air filter?

A clogged filter is a sneaky culprit. When airflow is choked off, the furnace can overheat and shut itself off on its safety limit — so it looks broken when it just needs a clean filter. If yours is gray and packed with dust, swap it and try again. In a busy household, check it monthly during heating season.

4. Is the furnace door fully closed?

Most furnaces have a safety switch that won't let the unit run unless the front door or access panel is latched shut. If someone changed the filter recently, the panel may not be seated. Press it firmly closed and listen for the furnace to kick on.

Why is my furnace blowing cold air instead of heat?

If the furnace runs but the air is cold, the usual causes are: the thermostat fan set to ON (see above), an ignition or pilot light failure, a dirty flame sensor, or the unit overheating and cutting the burners while the blower keeps running. The thermostat fix is DIY; the ignition and flame-sensor issues need a technician.

When should you call a pro?

Stop the DIY checks and call a licensed HVAC technician if:

  • You smell gas — leave the house and call from outside
  • The breaker keeps tripping
  • There's still no heat after the checks above
  • It's freezing out — in a Chicago cold snap, a home without heat can drop into the 40s inside and put your pipes at risk of freezing and bursting

Frequently asked questions

Why is my furnace not turning on?
Usually a wrong or low-battery thermostat, a tripped breaker, a clogged filter causing an overheat shutdown, an open furnace door, or a pilot/ignitor failure. Check the thermostat, breaker, filter, and door first.
Why is my furnace blowing cold air?
Most often the thermostat fan is set to ON instead of AUTO, so the blower runs without heat. It can also mean an ignition problem or the unit overheating and shutting its burners — those need a technician.
Should I reset my furnace if it won't turn on?
You can try one reset: thermostat down, breaker off 30 seconds then on, and furnace door closed. If it still won't start or trips again, call a licensed pro instead of repeatedly resetting.
When should I call a professional for no heat?
Right away if you smell gas, the breaker trips repeatedly, or there's no heat in freezing weather. Lost heat in a Chicago winter can freeze and burst pipes, so don't wait it out.
