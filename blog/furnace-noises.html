Strange Furnace Noises and What They Mean (Chicago Homeowner's Guide)

Updated November 2026·By Demiri Installs & Repairs·5 min read
Quick answer

Soft clicks, a quiet whoosh, and a steady blower hum are normal. A bang at startup usually means delayed ignition, squealing points to a belt or motor bearing, rattling to a loose panel or duct, and grinding to a failing motor — turn the furnace off and call a licensed HVAC pro for bangs, grinding, or any noise with a gas or burning smell.

Once the heat kicks on for the season in the Chicago suburbs, a lot of homeowners notice sounds they never heard in summer. Some are perfectly normal. Others are early warnings that a part is wearing out — and catching them in November is a lot better than losing heat in a January cold snap. Here's how to tell the difference.

What furnace noises are normal?

A healthy furnace isn't silent. You can expect:

  • A click when the thermostat calls for heat and the ignitor starts
  • A soft whoosh as the burners light
  • A steady hum from the blower moving air
  • Light ticking or popping from metal ducts as they warm up and cool down

The key is change: a sound that's new, louder than last year, or getting worse week to week deserves attention.

Why does my furnace bang or boom when it starts?

A loud bang right as the burners light is usually delayed ignition — gas collects for a split second before it catches, often because the burners are dirty. This isn't a DIY fix. Repeated mini-explosions stress the heat exchanger over time, so have a technician clean and inspect the burners. A lighter pop from the ductwork a minute or two after startup is usually just metal expanding and is generally harmless.

What does a squealing or screeching furnace mean?

High-pitched squealing usually comes from the blower: a worn or loose belt on older furnaces, or a motor bearing that needs lubrication or replacement on newer direct-drive units. It won't hurt you, but a motor that fails leaves you with no airflow — and no heat.

Why is my furnace rattling or grinding?

  • Rattling: often a loose access panel, screw, or duct section. Check that the furnace door is latched (it has a safety switch) and look for loose duct connections you can see.
  • Grinding or scraping metal: usually a failing motor bearing or a blower wheel hitting its housing. Turn the furnace off and call for service — running it can turn a repair into a replacement.

What about humming, buzzing, or repeated clicking?

A loud electrical buzz can point to a failing capacitor, transformer, or relay. Clicking that repeats without the burners lighting often means an ignition problem — the furnace keeps trying and failing to start. A dirty air filter can also make the blower strain and sound louder, so replacing a clogged filter is a good first step you can safely do yourself.

When should you call a pro?

Call a licensed HVAC technician if:

  • You hear banging at startup or grinding metal
  • Any noise comes with a gas smell (leave the house and call from outside) or a burning smell
  • The noise comes with weak or no heat, or the breaker trips
  • A new sound keeps getting louder

Frequently asked questions

Why does my furnace bang when it turns on?
Usually delayed ignition — gas builds up briefly before lighting, often due to dirty burners. Have a technician clean and inspect it. A softer pop from ducts expanding is typically harmless.
Is a squealing furnace dangerous?
Not immediately. It's usually a worn blower belt or a motor bearing. Left alone, the motor can fail and leave you without heat, so schedule a repair soon.
Is it normal for a furnace to make noise?
Yes — soft clicks, a quiet whoosh at ignition, blower hum, and light duct ticking are normal. New, loud, or worsening banging, grinding, screeching, or rattling should be checked.
When should I turn off my furnace because of a noise?
Turn it off and call a pro for grinding metal, repeated booms at startup, or any noise with a gas or burning smell or a tripping breaker. If you smell gas, leave and call from outside.
